# Legends Unbound — locked collection art direction

- Choose character scale and composition for the source and unique identity of each card; do not default to an oversized character, and keep the setting visible where it serves the scene.
- Plan the actual frame-break silhouette and depth around Cost and Power badges before generating art; avoid badge collisions and do not hide them with a foreground overlay that cuts the character apart. Check rest, motion and tilt extremes.

This is the **style source of truth** for every future card and all three evolutions. Pair it with the [asset and assembly contract](docs/CARD_ASSET_GUIDELINES.md). The current Tarzan, Sherlock and Dracula cards are the approved references for the shared **base-card** style and finish, not templates to copy literally. Every future base card must look like the same illustrated collection; separate card skins may be designed later and are not part of a new base card or evolution. When an asset conflicts with the locked style, revise the asset; do not change the shared style for an individual card.

## Fixed visual style

Create premium, illustrated fantasy-adventure collectible cards with the polished look of a hand-painted animated feature. Forms have a subtle 2.5D sense of depth: modeled volumes, convincing materials and dimensional lighting, while the result remains clearly illustrated. Use clean, purposeful silhouettes, controlled painterly brushwork, fine material texture and soft transitions between light and shadow. Keep the character immediately readable at phone card size.

- **Characters:** expressive, appealing, anatomically believable stylized proportions with clear eyes and distinctive silhouettes. Human characters should look like illustrated people, not photographs; adapt anatomy appropriately for creatures. Define the face, hair, hands and signature costume with care. Avoid grotesque distortion, exaggerated cartoon caricature and generic faces.
- **Materials and detail:** render cloth folds, hair, leather, metal and stone with tactile but restrained texture. Prioritize broad readable shapes first, then selective fine detail around the face, hands and signature object. Avoid noise, plastic gloss, flat vector fills, heavy comic halftones and busy edge detail.
- **Light and color:** use cinematic, directional key light, softly modeled shadows and a controlled warm/cool contrast, with a narrow rim light to separate the subject from the setting. Record the chosen light direction and palette in each card brief and keep them consistent across that card's layers and animation frames. Use rich, coherent colors and deep but readable shadows; reserve the brightest highlights for the focal subject and shared gold frame. Do not apply a blanket gold tint, neon glow or washed-out filter.
- **Composition and depth:** use a portrait 2:3 card face and a clear single-subject focal hierarchy, with a readable foreground character, supporting midground and atmospheric background. Keep the face and signature object unobstructed by stats and the large bottom title. Frame-break character portions are planned as separate transparent artwork; scenery and visual effects remain inside the card. Avoid baked-in perspective card mockups or cropped hands/faces.
- **Finish:** subtle cinematic depth and atmospheric separation, crisp important edges and softly painted background detail. Do not switch between anime, photorealism, pixel art, chibi, flat vector, comic-ink or a different 3D-render style from one card to another. Do not over-sharpen or render an image as a photograph of a physical card.

Always research the specific original public-domain stories, comics, cartoons or other source version before generation using the [character research workflow](docs/PUBLIC_DOMAIN_CHARACTER_RESEARCH.md). Interpret the sourced character with an original visual treatment and no actor likeness, protected later-adaptation design or reproduced logo. Names should be recognizable to the source, not invented themes. Use the existing approved color identities as references: Tarzan—emerald and sunlit gold; Sherlock—smoky blue and amber; Dracula—midnight violet and crimson. Other characters may have their own coherent palette while retaining the same finish, contrast and material rendering.

## Shared frame, title and evolution consistency

Keep the background and foreground at the same resting crop in all three levels. A fixed, size-aware overscan can cover the parallax travel; switching to Unbound must not introduce a separate zoom. Test the crop and edge coverage with zero tilt and at drag/sensor extremes.

Rarity is communicated **only by the border**. The four rarities are Common, Rare, Epic and Legendary. Legendary is rarest and uses a universal polished gold border. Epic uses a universal clean, solid purple frame without white dots, pale patches, frame sparkles or moving border sweeps. Purple evolution gems retain their own crystal shine. Rare uses a simpler shared amber-yellow frame with no border or evolution-gem animation. Common uses a simple static light-blue frame and matching blue gems. Never generate a separate border for each character or change character art, title, stats, evolution rules or animation tracks to indicate rarity. A rarity's border must work consistently for every character and all three evolution levels. Rarity and evolution are independent: the border communicates rarity, while its three diamond sockets communicate Origin, Awakened and Unbound.

Use the same shared gold frame asset for every Legendary card and level. Keep cost at top left, power at top right and a large, legible character-specific title at bottom center. Cost and power share matching dimensional, gold gemstone shields built into the frame artwork, with a blue cost stone, crimson power stone and separately rendered readable white numerals. The blue Cost and crimson Power shields remain static while the shared gold frame animates at every evolution level, respecting reduced motion. Wordmarks may draw on the source's visual history while retaining the collection's dimensional illustrated finish; do not reproduce an existing logo. Do not add other text inside the card. Frame, numbers and title are independent layers, never baked into character/environment art. The current Legendary front frame is `assets/cards/shared/frame-legendary-v6.png`; the Sherlock-inspired back is a shared prototype, not a per-character requirement.

The approved Legendary treatment is sculpted, multi-layered polished gold with sharp beveled corners and an integrated bottom-center evolution marker. Its living-gold finish has a visible slow breathing glow, horizontal light sweeps along the top and bottom rails and brighter moving reflections down the side edges, plus occasional frame glints and faceted pulses, staggered light sweeps and independently timed crystal starbursts on active evolution gems. A brighter, brief reveal accent accompanies selection, an evolution change or a return to the front. Keep the effects on the frame and gems, never on the stat shields or character; preserve the character's frame-break layer above the top sweep. Avoid visible rectangular shadows or background outside the frame. The [asset contract](docs/CARD_ASSET_GUIDELINES.md#legendary-border-rarity-and-effects) is the implementation reference.

Origin, Awakened and Unbound use **the same character, costume, pose, environment, title and shared frame artwork**. Do not generate different character art for an evolution. Origin is entirely inside the frame. Awakened reveals the same character beyond the top and side edges. Both allow a slight card-only left-right tilt, with no independent character or environment motion; the Legendary frame effects and rarity-specific gem effects remain active, while the Epic frame stays plain. Unbound retains that frame break and enables interactive parallax and the card's existing animation details; the bottom quarter of the character remains behind the frame. The shared frame's bottom-center three-diamond marker fills one, two or three cells for Origin, Awakened and Unbound respectively, with a gentle shine on active gems at every level. Every complete card requires exactly three distinct, independently authored animation pieces for Unbound, such as clothing, hair, props or localized magical or environmental effects. Shared frame or title effects and whole-image drifting do not count. Existing incomplete prototypes must be brought up to this requirement. The full behavior, title overflow and reduced-motion rules are defined in [the asset contract](docs/CARD_ASSET_GUIDELINES.md#one-character-three-evolutions).

## Reusable generation prompt

Use this fixed style block in every character, environment, foreground and animation-asset prompt. Add a card-specific brief after it; do not replace the style block with a different aesthetic.

> Legends Unbound collectible-card illustration, premium hand-painted animated-feature fantasy adventure, coherent stylized anatomy and expressive faces, clean readable silhouette, dimensional painterly shading, fine tactile material detail, cinematic directional light and restrained rim highlights, rich controlled color, readable deep shadows, atmospheric layered depth, polished 2.5D illustrated finish. Portrait 2:3 card composition, no card border, no text, no logos, no UI, no watermark; not a photo, anime, pixel art, flat vector, chibi, heavily inked comic or physical-card mockup.

Append: **character and source inspiration**, signature pose/costume/object, card-specific palette and light direction, asset role, crop/anchor, and whether the background must be opaque or genuinely transparent. Design one shared composition for all three levels. For an animation frame, also include the fixed reference canvas, unchanged attachment position, exact rest-frame registration and the one specific moving piece. Generate separate underpainting and animation pieces as described in the asset contract; a prompt alone does not guarantee alignment or clean transparency.

Before approving any new base card, compare the assembled card side by side with all three existing cards at the same desktop and small-phone card size. Check silhouette and face readability, character identity across evolutions, brushwork and material finish, light direction across layers, color/contrast balance, title and stat readability, and clean frame edges. Inspect every animation pose and the entire loop for detached pieces, inconsistent texture, ghosting or a style shift. Reject and revise mismatches before adding the package to the catalog. Device checks and pipeline validation remain required by the asset contract.

## Existing prototype generation notes

The current core art and transparent overlays were generated individually with the built-in image generation tool. The shared front frame is an image; the gems and their numbers are React Native components, and character titles are separate image assets. The existing characters use the same artwork across the implemented evolution levels.

The early card concepts used these directions:

- **Tarzan:** heroic jungle adventurer with long dark hair, a three-quarter leap, green and gold rim lighting, and an emerald jungle with a waterfall and sun shafts.
- **Sherlock Holmes:** Victorian detective with a tailored dark coat, windblown scarf and magnifying glass, with cool blue shadows and warm amber light against London rooftops and gas lamps.
- **Dracula:** vampire aristocrat with a black and crimson cape, expressive outstretched hand, violet and crimson light, and a moonlit gothic castle and storm clouds.

Foreground and effect assets were generated separately. The shared Legendary frame is sculpted polished gold with angular beveled corners, a transparent center and three integrated obsidian sockets, without character-specific motifs. Faceted obsidian sockets are built into the frame and the dimensional active gem is a shared overlay selected by rarity: gold for Legendary, purple for Epic. It sits above the environment; the character can appear above it only where the evolution's frame-break rule permits.

The current shared reverse is a text-free Victorian detective design with a central magnifying glass, clue motifs, brass engraving and a midnight-blue palette. It is a shared prototype back for the current collection, not a requirement to generate a new back for each character.

## Character-specific pose and assets

Research each character's original public-domain appearance and choose a distinctive, recognizable pose based on their personality, source and iconic actions. Plan a readable silhouette and suitable top/side frame break and parallax for that pose; a hand reaching toward the viewer is only one possible choice and must not become a collection template. If the original source has no clear iconic pose, design an original, character-appropriate one and record that choice.

Each new card needs its own planned and generated character, background, foreground, environmental elements, particles, effects, title and other card-specific art layers. Do not borrow another character's scenery or overlay layers. Generate as many distinct, registered assets as the composition and its three animation plans need; there is no arbitrary image-count limit. Shared rarity borders, gems, back and other universal card UI remain shared. Optimize and test the resulting package on target devices; an unlimited generation allowance is not a runtime performance budget.

Plan exactly three character-specific animation tracks before generating any card art. Seek a different combination and appropriate techniques for each character instead of repeating a scarf, a prop shine and another movement as a formula. For female characters, consider hair movement when the design and story support it, but do not force it if clean separation or quality cannot be achieved. Every track must pass the existing motion and seam quality gates.
