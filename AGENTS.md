# Legends Unbound project memory

- Choose character scale and composition for the source and unique identity of each card; do not default to an oversized character, and keep the setting visible where it serves the scene.
- Plan the actual frame-break silhouette and depth around Cost and Power badges before generating art; avoid badge collisions and do not hide them with a foreground overlay that cuts the character apart. Check rest, motion and tilt extremes.

For a name-only card request, follow [the card generation pipeline](docs/CARD_GENERATION_PIPELINE.md), [art direction](ART_DIRECTION.md), [asset guidelines](docs/CARD_ASSET_GUIDELINES.md), and [Jane Porter lessons](docs/JANE_PORTER_LESSONS.md). These are repository instructions for future card work, not a claim that the pipeline is automated.

- Research the named character's original public-domain source and document the version and jurisdiction. Choose their own source-appropriate iconic pose, not a default reaching hand. Preserve the established collection art style; each character needs their own distinctive title and newly generated scene, foreground, effect and particle art.
- Choose each card's pose from its own story, personality or a compelling action/moment; poses can be quiet, dramatic, unusual or dynamic, and must not default to the same standing, facing or reaching composition. Choose lighting from the actual scene and character, with motivated sources and coherent layer lighting. Sunbeams and sunlight are optional creative choices, not a default requirement.
- Creative pose, camera distance, framing, scale, lighting and palette are open choices for each character: use whatever best expresses the original source and scene. Sunlight is welcome when appropriate; there is no mandate to use or avoid it. Preserve established functional evolution, border and UI behavior while making those creative choices.
- Before generating anything, record the complete composition, layer/occlusion plan and exactly three distinct, character-specific Unbound animation tracks. Seek variety across characters in both ideas and techniques. Consider clean hair movement for female characters when appropriate, without forcing it. Generate as many registered assets as the design needs; optimize and measure the runtime result.
- Animation is a prerequisite to the base card, not a task to retrofit afterward: before final base-card generation, plan and produce the three motion-ready asset prototypes, their masks, rest layers and hidden underpaint as needed, and demonstrate that each intended movement can be clean and visible. Use disposable studies to establish registration; if any track fails, revise the design and repeat this gate before generating the final base. Preserve the final shared rest art across levels. The per-card planner and reviewer checklists in the pipeline require recorded evidence for each track.
- Treat each animation as a chance to express this character, its setting and story. Explore and justify different techniques, including custom masks, deformable rigs, authored frames, flow maps, particles and layered occlusion where appropriate; do not reuse a generic three-effect recipe or add effects merely to reach the count. Measure feasibility and performance on the current renderer and reject techniques that fail visual or device checks.
- Before choosing the three tracks, brainstorm multiple distinct character-specific visual and motion approaches, compare them with existing cards and each other, and record why the selected ideas and techniques serve this character. Be open to new masks, rigs, simulated or procedural effects, authored artwork and other feasible methods; do not turn sprites, any one technique, or a successful prior card into a default. Creativity is guided by story and measured execution, not novelty for its own sake.
- For future card titles, plan a unique dimensional wordmark with convincing 3D depth, bevel, material and lighting that belongs to the character and remains legible at phone size. The user said current titles are fine; do not change existing title art merely to apply this future direction.
- The user praised Korak's distinct color, pose and animation variety. Preserve this creative process as an example, not a style or effects template for later characters.
- La of Opar's user feedback praised the distinctive, planned animation design; use its story-driven combination and pre-base studies as an example of the process, not a recipe to copy. Her wheel, painted fire frames and hanging pendant are still subject to the quality and device gates recorded in her plan.
- Make each track clearly noticeable at real phone card size while remaining slow, smooth and natural. Check full loops and transitions for seams, cuts, duplicate pixels, ghosting, detached anchors, clipping and underpaint gaps; an imperceptible or defective track fails. Never claim an unfinished card is complete.
- Use the same art in all levels: Origin inside the frame and static, Awakened top/side character frame break and static, Unbound frame break plus parallax and three card-specific animations. Keep the bottom quarter behind the frame and all environment inside. Preserve reduced motion, preloading and lifecycle behavior.
- Keep the background and foreground rest framing consistent across evolution levels. Use only the size-aware overscan needed to cover interactive parallax, not a new Unbound-only zoom; verify coverage at tilt extremes.
- Background size, crop and composition may be adjusted per card whenever the scene needs it: the character must sit, stand or make contact naturally with its supporting environment. The consistency rule applies across that card's evolution levels; it is not a ban on fitting or resizing a new scene. Check the contact points and tilt extremes in the actual composition.
- Rarity is expressed by the shared border and its integrated gems, not by per-character art. Tarzan, Sherlock and Dracula use Legendary gold; Jane uses the clean Epic purple frame and purple gems; Sabor uses the shared simpler amber-yellow Rare frame with static border and gems. Common uses a simple, static light-blue border. Do not change Legendary visuals when adjusting other rarities.
- Verify the actual web rendering at desktop and phone sizes, including all levels, frame, title, stats, flip, selection and loading. Physical Android sensor/performance validation and the previously reported crash and horizontal line remain pending user ADB setup. Do not build an APK unless requested.
- Treat performance as part of every change: avoid duplicate decoding, unnecessary updates and inactive rendering, and profile representative interactions. Preserve the requested visual quality, preload and transitions; report measurements and untested devices honestly.
- Finish each card with the [strict player-perspective review](docs/PLAYER_REVIEW_PROMPT.md). Fix and re-review actionable defects, report unverified checks, and incorporate real user feedback; do not claim the user is impressed or satisfied without their acceptance.
