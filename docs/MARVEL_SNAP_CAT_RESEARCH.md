# Marvel Snap card art research and Legends Unbound pipeline proposal

Research date: 2026-11-19. This is a reference and an implementation proposal, not a description of a delivered CAT clone. Read it alongside [card generation pipeline](CARD_GENERATION_PIPELINE.md), [asset contract](CARD_ASSET_GUIDELINES.md), [animation techniques](CARD_ANIMATION_TECHNIQUES.md), and [locked art direction](../ART_DIRECTION.md). ## What public sources actually establish

- [Unity's Marvel Snap case study](https://unity.com/resources/marvel-snap) says Second Dinner's internal Card Art Tool (CAT) lets artists sculpt, heightmap, animate and add parallax in real time in the Unity Editor; it describes frame break, sharing the tool with external artists, and artist-controlled grouping of card art and VFX for Addressables. The case study describes mobile downloading and unloading remote assets and separate content builds. These are confirmed reported workflow capabilities, not a public CAT specification.
- [Engineer Ali Wallick's project account](https://aliwallick.com/projects/marvel-snap) independently describes the artist-facing card art tool and an in-game card detail view with animation/effect preview. [Marvel's art interview](https://www.marvel.com/articles/games/inside-the-art-of-marvel-snap) discusses the combination of art, 3D effects and animation and character-specific frame breaks. [Marvel Snap's upgrade FAQ](https://marvelsnap.helpshift.com/hc/en/3-marvel-snap/faq/34-what-does-upgrading-a-card-do/?p=all%2F1000) describes observable frame break, parallax, animation, logo and border effects.
- None of those sources specifies a single universal Uber-shader, exact texture channel meanings, exact depth formula, number of draw calls or layers per card, how all individual character animations are authored, particle counts, or that all cards avoid frame-by-frame animation. The supplied formulas and particle recipes are **possible engineering approaches**, not verified facts about Second Dinner's internals. A third-party sample or an image filename cannot establish an engine's private shader contract. Do not repeat the user's pasted text's exact-implementation claims as fact.
- [Official March 2024 patch notes](https://marvelsnap.com/patch-notes-mar-12-2024/) list new VFX on Deadpool, Psylocke, Legion, Darkhawk and Moon Girl; [June 2024 notes](https://marvelsnap.com/patch-notes-june-25-2024/) list changed VFX on Death, Knull, and Black Cat; [October 2026 notes](https://marvelsnap.com/patch-notes-october-20-2026/) report VFX/SFX additions for Ravonna Renslayer, Shadow King, Dracula, Hellcow and Ronan. These sources establish *that VFX were added or changed*, not their precise appearance or implementation. They do not make an exhaustive catalogue of every variant's animation.
- Official [November 2026 notes](https://marvelsnap.com/patch-notes-november-17-2026/) identify different **gameplay-event** cases: repeated Hawkeye On Reveal timing, Shadow King power-change feedback, and Hellcow cape VFX clipping during hover/drag. [June 2025 notes](https://marvelsnap.com/patch-notes-june-24-2025/) discuss anchored target effects, board-edge clipping, VFX persistence and white-tile failures. These are distinct from a card's idle art loop, and their reported fixes do not disclose shaders. Use them as testing lessons, not as claims that our game has those mechanics.
- The official [finishes/flares description](https://marvelsnap.com/fntyspltlglpg/) names observable finish and flare categories including Foil, Prism, Ink, Gold, Tone, Glimmer, Stardust, Krackle and Embers. These are references for visual categories, not instructions to copy Marvel cosmetics or their rarity rules. Legends Unbound retains its own art, rarity and evolution rules.
- Distinguish actual in-game card art from promotional cinematics: [Mana Animation's Deadpool and Wolverine project](https://www.mana.tv/work/marvel-snap-deadpool-and-wolverine-animation) explicitly describes a seasonal promotional animation using frame-by-frame cel work. It does **not** prove how Snap's runtime cards animate. Do not treat a cinematic, an extracted thumbnail or a fan reconstruction as source code.

Future reference reviews should capture the exact card **and variant**, screen/context, evolution/finish, observed idle/drag/reveal behavior, capture date, and a link to a primary source or a personally observed recording. ## Proposed per-card package and tool contract

Do not force the same number of visual files or exactly 3 PNGs. Choose enough planes for true occlusion and depth, and exactly three **animation tracks** for a finished card. One track may use multiple layers, masks or emitters. Package the same approved rest artwork for all levels. Recommended authoring roles, only when needed:

1. Opaque backplate with painted overscan/bleed and complete background behind subject; optional independently painted far/mid/near planes, clouds or debris.
2. Separate occluding parts: rear cloth/hair, torso, hands/weapon, foreground limbs and effects; isolate only elements that actually need different depth or motion. Preserve underpainting behind displaced elements. A flat image plus a height map cannot recover hidden pixels.
3. Mask/anchor files: motion influence, depth/height, emission, metallic response, silhouette/occlusion and effect shapes as required. Reuse a map only when resolution, coordinate space, sampling and update cadence are compatible. Retain editable source maps even if runtime channels are packed later.
4. Title front/back or glint mask when actually needed, with our shared rarity-specific frame, readable numbers and evolution gems remaining separate. Origin must fit everything inside; Awakened permits only top/side character frame break; Unbound adds the three approved tracks, interactive parallax and allowed title/background effects. No environmental frame break or character shimmer.
5. Track definitions: piece ID, type, parent plane/anchor, bounds, depth and occlusion, mask(s), stable rest pose, underpaint, parameters, loop duration/phase, visible-area and overflow limits, fallback, and automated/manual test evidence. Record asset ownership/provenance and versions. No attached effect may appear from nowhere on Unbound; its registered rest appearance belongs to all levels.

## Technique library: evaluate, don't assume

| Candidate | Practical recipe for our future pipeline | Quality gates / constraints |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Variable depth planes** | Give illustrated planes individual depth and conservative tilt response; prepaint hidden regions and overscan. | The user's Origin/Awakened artwork remains flat together; only Unbound gains layer parallax. Avoid cut edges, visible empty borders and too many independently animated full-screen views. |
| **Height-field surface parallax** | Artist paints grayscale depth for a surface; sample it to offset UVs as tilt changes, or deform a sufficiently tessellated mesh. Test on an isolated object first. | Height values need calibration, stable silhouette and occlusion; UV offsets can pull from unrelated pixels and reveal holes. A flat quad cannot really reconstruct unseen geometry. Do not distort face, text, gloves or stat shields. This is a candidate, not implemented. |
| **Masked cloth/hair** | Isolate the piece and underpaint. A weighted mesh with anchored vertices, or a limited UV wave weighted by a painted root-to-tip mask, can produce distinct bends. | A UV wave on one flattened image moves texture only; it does not create a correct moving silhouette or missing background. Preserve root, hands and face. Compare with hand-authored in-betweens when a rig looks rubbery. |
| **Water / cloud / fire flow** | Use registered moving texture or a tileable flow/noise map within an artist-painted effect region. Use two offset passes with covered handoffs, or a true periodic distortion. Maintain stable source, destination and rest. | Never repeat visibly, move cliffs, leave rectangular boundaries, flash, appear from nowhere, or hide behind artwork. Tarzan's existing waterfall is a prototype with a separate smaller-flow region, not a general solution. |
| **Localized emission** | A painted emission mask can drive a constrained glow, color and rhythm where a character's existing object warrants it; an HDR+bloom stack is one optional implementation. | No character-wide shimmer. A floating light must have a static/rest counterpart in Origin/Awakened where required. HDR targets, bloom passes, tone mapping and screen-space spill cost memory/bandwidth and require engine/device support; a value greater than 1 alone does not guarantee bloom. |
| **Effects / particles** | Small reusable, parameterized templates, parented to the right depth and anchor, can support sparks, rain, fire or debris. Allow authored textures, spawn regions, deterministic seeds and bounded lifetimes. | Do not add random bats, glow, smoke, drifting scenery or generic ambient motion. Effects need a justified pre-existing rest appearance across levels, must be one of the three planned tracks if counted, respect frame clipping, remain legible on mobile and pass the same anti-pop quality gate. Event-only board VFX are a different future subsystem. |
| **Foil / glints** | Keep existing shared frame and Unbound title/background effects; optionally expose authored material masks/tilt-responsive lighting on permitted non-character surfaces. | Legendary border is gold and identical across characters; stat shields stay static, no rectangular glow, and the character remains free of shimmer. These effects never substitute for three character-specific tracks. |

The supplied tilt pseudocode also needs correction: `rotationRateUnbiased` is angular **velocity**, not an absolute tilt angle; using it directly as orientation does not reproduce a held pose, while integration can drift. The current `useDeviceTilt.ts` instead calibrates a device-orientation reading, smooths and clamps it, falls back to acceleration or touch, and recalibrates after app foregrounding. Sensor availability and platform/browser permission behavior still need device validation.

The pasted proposals for continuous whole-background pan/zoom, breathing camera, and generic ambient motion conflict with this project's accepted direction and are **not adopted**. The user's earlier rejected Dracula bats and crimson effect demonstrate why inserting an attractive preset without a registered rest pose and matching composition fails. Sine waves, noise textures, glows and particles are optional tools, not automatically acceptable animation. Tilt-driven card interaction is not ambient animation.

### Candidate effect recipes for future experiments

These are independent engineering recipes derived from the user's briefs and standard graphics practice, **not verified descriptions of Marvel Snap's private shaders**. First choose a card-specific visual reason and a single approved existing rest composition; then use the least complicated method that passes our gates. Every example is optional and needs an artist-authored mask, anchor, depth, fallback and actual-device measurement.

- **Depth-weighted tilt:** for a surface texture, one candidate samples a depth field `h` and computes a bounded offset such as `sampleUV = uv + tilt × (h - neutralDepth) × strength`. The sign and units depend on the coordinate and sampling convention; calibrate in an editor. Use separate underpainted planes for hard silhouettes, fists and weapons rather than expecting an offset texture to invent occluded pixels. Limit sampling to padded UVs, protect face/text, and compare all extreme directions. A painted grayscale map is not itself geometry.
- **Root-to-tip fabric/hair:** in a shader spike, sample a smooth weight mask (0 at root, increasing toward the tip) and modulate a bounded wave by position and time. A weighted mesh or authored frames can move the true contour, while a single UV displacement only moves pixels inside an existing surface; preserve transparent overscan and prepaint what movement reveals. Show a weight overlay and pin roots in the preview. Try it against Tarzan's anchored strip control, and reject elastic or detached results.
- **Water and other flowing textures:** author a directional region/mask, a tileable flow field or a registered texture with bleed. For a two-pass loop, offset the passes by half a cycle and arrange their coverage so each wrap is hidden by the other, rather than fading both away at once. Prefer periodic/noise-distorted motion over a repeated recognizable motif. Keep waterfall lip, rocks, trees and mist anchored. For distant smaller streams use independent conservative motion, as in the current Tarzan prototype. Verify all pixel masks, two handoffs and real-time cadence; mathematical continuity alone is not visual proof.
- **Flame, smoke and heat haze:** an optional engine prototype can scroll a seamless noise field through an authored silhouette or flow map; shape it with a fixed source/anchor, periodic phase and a controlled color ramp. A separate bounded distortion can make heat ripple the intended region. The flame/smoke must belong in the approved rest art at all levels, with motion limited to Unbound. Avoid large moving translucent rectangles, moving nearby characters, random opacity pops and generic idle background drift. A few authored frame sequences may look better and cost less than extra shader passes.
- **Electric arcs:** candidate methods include a short registered in-between sequence, or seeded tileable noise thresholded into thin moving lines with softened edges. Animate a deterministic repeatable phase or event trigger; constrain branches to a designed path, keep a stable source, avoid unmotivated flashes, and test readability/sensitivity. Don't assert a threshold/noise shader is how any named Marvel card was built.
- **Emissive accents / magical runes:** paint a separate localized influence mask on an allowed part of an existing prop or environment; drive color and brightness with restrained curves. An HDR bloom backend needs a compatible high-range intermediate, threshold, blur passes and tone mapping; the app currently provides none of that pipeline. An image/gradient with a clipped soft halo can be a cheaper comparison, not physically equivalent HDR. Do not blanket-shimmer skin or the whole character, alter cost/power shields, or add a glowing object only on Unbound.
- **Metal/glass/foil:** use a material mask and tilt-derived highlight or a preauthored reflection on a permitted non-character surface. Keep the current identity and Legendary frame, make idle and input-driven components independent, feather the rail boundaries and avoid glowing stat shields. Foil is a shared presentation effect, not one of a card's three tracks.
- **Particles and small moving accents:** parameterize spawn region, color, density, size/lifetime ranges, seed, motion, z-layer, parent, clipping and rest appearance. Split back/front systems only when actual occlusion improves the composition and the environment remains inside the frame. A reusable source atlas or tiny sprite sequence may help, but do not assume four frames or a fixed emitter count is sufficient. Budget per effect, pool if measurements justify it, and test no lingering particles on card switch, flip, app background or reveal cancellation. Effects not present in the shared resting art need a design revision before use in an Unbound-only track.

For any of these, keep continuous card-art loops separate from stateful board VFX. If future gameplay adds effects that travel to a target or repeat, drive them from explicit events with cancellation, deterministic anchors, priorities and timeout/reduced-motion alternatives. The official Hawkeye/Hellcow fixes in the examples show why timing and clipping matter, but do not dictate a runtime design for this project.

## Optional packed masks and rendering backends

A packed texture can hold up to four independent scalar channels, but **channel assignment is our own versioned contract**, not a Marvel Snap fact. For a trial only: e.g. R = height, G = motion weight, B = localized emission, A = non-character material response. Put alpha/coverage for the artwork in its own art texture; do not silently overload the coverage channel. Separate maps or separate per-layer packed maps may be better when domains, resolutions, texture filtering, UVs or update rates differ. An artwork may need more than one influence or more than one map. Record neutral/default values, color-space, mask polarity, channel ownership, expected resolution, alpha mode, allowed compression and fallback. [Unity's channel-packing documentation](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/urp/shaders-in-universalrp-channel-packed-texture.html) is a general example, not evidence of Snap's exact mapping.

Masks are numeric data: sample in linear/non-color space, not as sRGB color. Verify that the actual PNG/import path does not premultiply RGB using the data channel stored in alpha; ordinary UI image pipelines can damage independent channels. Check edge padding, filtering, mipmaps, texture compression, seams and precision on each target, and compare decoded channel values to the authoring source. [Unity's texture guidance](https://docs.unity3d.com/kr/2022.3/ScriptReference/TextureImporter-sRGBTexture.html) explains why non-color data is treated differently from color. A channel-packed file is not necessarily smaller in GPU memory than an optimized single-channel texture, and a mask on a different UV layout may require another sample anyway. Benchmark memory and quality; do not assume four masks are always cheaper.

A master material with parameterized feature modules is an architecture candidate, but a checkbox does **not** automatically make disabled GPU work free. Dynamic branches, extra samples, transparent overdraw and offscreen passes can still cost; static variants can multiply compile time, package size and runtime warm-up. [Unity's shader-conditional guidance](https://docs.unity3d.com/cn/2021.3/Manual/shader-conditionals.html) describes the tradeoff. Prefer a small set of measured specialized material variants, meaningful defaults, and fixed budgets over an unbounded monolithic shader. Cache material/pipeline state and group work only after profiling; one shader per card is not the same thing as one draw call or one GPU pass.

Potential implementation paths, all **unimplemented and subject to a benchmark spike**:

- **Current Reanimated + SVG**: lowest immediate migration; good for separate planes, anchored pieces and bounded masks, but native SVG's installed turbulence/displacement primitives are unimplemented and full-size mask/image layers may be expensive.
- **Expo GL / WebGL**: custom shader, mesh and texture control; [Expo SDK 58 GLView docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/gl-view/) document platform APIs, worklet constraints and older-device WebGL2 limitations. Prototype one shared renderer/context, not one unmeasured context per card. Validate transparency, masking, native frame pacing, context loss/resume, texture uploads and memory.
- **React Native Skia or another supported native renderer**: possible alternative for meshes/runtime effects and compositing; evaluate library/version compatibility with this Expo/React Native setup, web support and build size before selecting it. This project does not depend on it today.
- **Unity/Godot**: a separate engine migration, not a shader patch for the current app. Compare toolchain, bridge integration, download and build costs, portability, artist workflow and device tests before any architectural commitment. Neither CAT nor Snap's code can simply be imported.

## Staged implementation roadmap for a future name-only request

0. **Protect current behavior and measure baseline.** Record reference stills, moving captures and traces for all current cards, levels, tilt extremes, flip, reduced motion, loading and app resume. On representative low/mid/high Android and iOS phones measure frame-time distribution (including p95/p99), missed frames, GPU/CPU time, texture/mask memory, cold/warm load, APK/app size and thermal effects. Keep the current frame and visual/evolution rules. Establish numeric acceptance budgets from measurements and product targets, not unsupported claims.
1. **Versioned package + validator first.** Specify layer/track schema and exactly-three-plan gate; generate TS static `require` registry from it; validate file existence, size/aspect, transparency, masks, channels, coverage, overdraw candidates, clipping, underpaint, manifest graph and readiness. Provide deterministic fallback and descriptive errors. Migrate current cards without changing pixels, interaction or loading semantics; keep the current implementation until parity tests pass.
2. **CAT-inspired internal preview, not a CAT clone.** Build an artist-facing dev preview that loads a local package, toggles/solos/reorders planes, overlays masks/depth/anchors, previews parallax and loop scrubbing, toggles all evolutions and reduced motion, shows actual phone sizes and tilt extremes, runs a seam/composite diff, reports estimated assets and loads, and exports a versioned package/report. Original/full-res edit sources stay external or appropriately versioned. Preserve per-card identity; do not hard-code five planes or copy Agent 13.
3. **First backend spike: one effect, one existing card.** Use the accepted Tarzan waterfall as the control: compare current dual-sample masks against a truly periodic, masked flow or textured mesh prototype. Same art, rest appearance, frame rules, selected/front lifecycle and identical gesture inputs. Check all cascade boundaries, lower falls, loop frames and slow real-time playback; prove no rocks/trees move, no seam, no ghost and visible improvement at phone size. Benchmark both at equal quality on real Android/iOS and web, including scene with multiple visible cards. Keep the existing renderer if the spike fails.
4. **Then trial one separated cloth/hair piece.** Start from a clean isolated asset and underpaint; compare a weighted mesh against registered authored frames and the current Tarzan two-joint strip. Root and occluders fixed, complete rest pose identical at every level. Reject any seam/doubling/flat rigid bend. Do not automatically replace approved existing animation with an unvalidated shader.
5. **Conditional expansion.** Only after parity, accessibility and measured device wins: evaluate packed maps, specialized shader variants, localized emission and a small artist-controlled effects library. Add a fallback per technique. Integrate deterministic package builds, signed/versioned delivery and offline cache only when catalog scale and product needs justify it; preserve ready-before-reveal and explicit user authorization for new network-delivery policy. Reassess whether preloading *all* assets remains feasible before changing the user's current preload-all requirement.
6. **Complete three meaningful tracks per card.** Continue Tarzan's separately planned hair only after isolation and underpainting pass. Sherlock and Dracula also remain incomplete. Nothing in this research makes a card complete by counting shared foil, particles, parallax or a failed track.

### Required checks for a finished card or renderer spike

- Layer/underpaint registration at original size and 200–400%; no moving stationary scenery, holes, doubled silhouettes, seams, cuts, color shifts or rectangular mask edges. Check silhouette and all tilt extremes, including down edges, frame corners and the bottom-quarter border.
- Multiple full loops and loop boundary in slow motion **and real time** at a small phone card; enough in-between poses and natural movement, not an opacity-only flash. Test actual visible motion against character occlusion and title/stats, while comparing Origin/Awakened same still pose.
- Selected/unselected, front/back, evolution switching, app background/resume, reduced motion, sensor recalibration, drag, repeated interactions, loading/error/retry and offline bundled fallback. No secondary effect popping into view on Unbound.
- Main/shader pass and texture/mask instrumentation, overdraw, compression artifacts, alpha, thermal and memory on representative physical Android and iOS; compare Web separately. Record test device, OS, resolution, time and results. Do not infer native correctness or performance from browser capture or an APK build.
