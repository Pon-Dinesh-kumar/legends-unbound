# Card animation techniques and implementation notes

This is a reference for choosing and planning animation techniques in the [name-to-card pipeline](CARD_GENERATION_PIPELINE.md). For external references, the Agent 13 layer observations, and the proposed CAT-inspired editor/renderer, see [Marvel Snap CAT research](MARVEL_SNAP_CAT_RESEARCH.md). The [asset contract](CARD_ASSET_GUIDELINES.md) and [art direction](../ART_DIRECTION.md) remain authoritative. These notes describe the current prototype, not an automatic card generator or proof that a technique will work for every image.

## Plan before producing assets

For each new card, plan **exactly three distinct card-specific animated pieces together before generating assets**. Record the visual purpose, rest pose, source coordinates, parent layer and attachment, foreground/background occlusion, motion path, timing, required masks and underpainting, expected mobile visibility, and fallback if isolation fails. Shared frame or title effects, gyro/parallax, moving an entire illustration, and opacity-only flashes do not count as the three pieces. All three pieces have the same registered rest appearance at every evolution: Origin is inside the frame and still; Awakened adds the same artwork's top/side frame break; only the selected, visible Unbound front unlocks card-specific motion and independent parallax. Respect reduced motion.

Choose the technique per piece, not by a fixed template:

| Technique | Current example | Good fit | Do not use it as a shortcut when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Registered art with localized shape frames | Sherlock's scarf and Dracula's cape | A movable fabric piece can be separated, anchored and underpainted cleanly. | Crossfades ghost, attachment drifts, a hand/face changes, or a rigid rotation pretends to be fabric deformation. Add authored in-betweens or a suitable rig and re-review. |
| Two-segment raster rig | Tarzan's waist-strip prototype | An isolated strip lies over transparent space, has a stable anchor and bends convincingly at a joint. | Its cut intersects the body, the joint shows a seam or ghost, or rigid motion looks unnatural. Use finished underpainting and authored in-betweens or a weighted rig instead. |
| Masked moving samples of the existing background | Tarzan's waterfall prototype | An amorphous, continuously moving natural texture has enough usable source detail and can be isolated without moving rocks or foliage. | The mask moves stationary features, a seam or repeated texture is visible, water falls outside its painted path, or movement cannot be seen at card size. Prefer a purpose-built registered loop or a proven deformation/flow rig. |
| Authored frame sequence | Planned option, not yet a generic runtime | A piece needs changing silhouette, distinct folds, an anchored root and precise occlusion. | Frames are unrelated generations crossfaded over an unchanged original, or the rest/underpainting and registration have not passed review. |
| Textured mesh or bone rig | Candidate, not implemented | Hair, cloth or other pieces need continuous weighted bending. | There is no working cross-platform authoring/runtime pipeline or the weights expose seams. Spike, test and measure before adopting a dependency. |

Reject cuts, ghosting, visible resets, duplicated artwork, detached pieces, moving rocks, inconsistent light, gaps, and motion that is imperceptible at target size. A mathematically continuous parameter is not proof of a visually seamless loop. Do not claim a card is complete just to meet a track count. Dracula's [redesign notes](DRACULA_ANIMATION_REDESIGN.md) document rejected cuff and separation experiments; [Tarzan's plan](TARZAN_ANIMATION_PLAN.md) records the waterfall and two unimplemented candidates.

## Tarzan: localized waterfall flow

**Purpose and scope.** `src/components/TarzanWaterfall.tsx` is a card-specific prototype for the waterfall already painted in `assets/cards/tarzan/background.png`. It reuses the existing opaque 1024 × 1536 background; no generated loop frames or replacement rest art are added. The original background is the still rest image in every evolution. Moving samples of that same image are masked over it in Unbound, so inspect closely for stationary features creeping into the mask and for crossfade ghosting. This approach is not a fluid simulation and is not suitable for changing silhouettes or attached character parts.

**Registration and depth.** Regions are defined in the background's source coordinate system as overlapping feathered radial ellipses: one group follows the main and intermediate cascade; a separate group covers the three smaller lower falls. Both web and native use the same coordinates and alpha falloff. Web uses an encoded SVG as a CSS mask; native uses `MaskedView` and `react-native-svg`. Mask geometry must match the image's `cover` fit, including its aspect ratio and crop. The component is placed *inside the existing animated background view* in `LayeredCard.tsx`, after the original background image and before foreground scenery, background foil, and character layers. It therefore shares the background's parallax, stays behind Tarzan and foliage, and is clipped by the art window and `FrameInterior`. Never place it in screen coordinates or above the character/frame.

**Continuous handoff.** A Reanimated shared phase `p` advances linearly from 0 to 1 in 5 seconds and repeats. Each region displays two registered samples of the same background:

- The bottom sample stays fully opaque and moves downward by `(p - 0.5) × distance`.
- The top sample moves downward at the same rate, half a cycle out of phase: `(((p + 0.5) % 1) - 0.5) × distance`. Its opacity is `cos²(πp)`.
- At the bottom sample's wrap, the top sample is fully opaque and covers it. At the top sample's wrap, its opacity is zero and the opaque bottom sample covers it. Full coverage avoids intermittently revealing the stationary background during the handoff. The cosine fade has zero slope at its extrema; both resets are hidden in the composition.
- The main flow uses `distance = cardWidth × 0.18`; the shorter lower falls use `cardWidth × 0.06`, keeping their source and mist less disturbed. Treat these as artwork-specific starting values, not universal constants. Tune speed and distance together at actual card size, including both ends of the loop.

The two regions currently draw four image views from the same source, inside two masks. This can increase overdraw and native memory/GPU cost; source reuse and preloading do not prove a device can render it smoothly. Test the actual mask alignment and phase handoff on web, Android and iOS. `react-native-svg` and native masking behavior need device verification, not just browser verification. If the two sampled images look like ghosted doubles or shift static features, redesign the source/mask or use authored continuous frames or a suitable rig.

**Lifecycle and loading.** `LayeredCard` supplies `active` only for the selected Unbound front with motion enabled; `TarzanWaterfall` also checks `AppState`. It cancels and resets its phase and returns no animated overlay when inactive, on the back, in the background or with reduced motion. Origin and Awakened continue to show the original still image. The waterfall uses the already preloaded background asset and its existing readiness/error handling; the overlay images also report errors. If a future technique introduces separate masks, textures or frames, add each to `src/hooks/preloadCardAssets.ts` and the named mounted-layer readiness requirements in `App.tsx`, and retain error/retry handling. Avoid claiming that a downloaded asset has been decoded on every platform.

## Tarzan: anchored waist-strip rig

`src/components/TarzanCharacter.tsx` is an experimental two-section rig using the **original** `character.png`, with no extra raster assets. The selected strip extends from the waist into transparent space beside the knife. An SVG luminance mask removes just its source-coordinate contour from the base; two clips redraw the same pixels with a five-source-pixel overlap at their joint. The upper part pivots near the belt, and the lower part bends around a second anchor. Both are nested in the normal character/parallax transform and are present at the same rest coordinates in all evolutions. A single shared six-second phase drives both the upper frame-breaking and lower clipped copies, avoiding asynchronous seams. The phase runs only on the selected visible Unbound front while the app is foregrounded, and is reset for reduced motion. The original background remains visible beneath the separated strip; a generated underpaint was rejected because it changed unrelated artwork.

This is suitable only when a movable region can be cut out over genuine transparent space and connected without changing the rest composition. The browser rest comparison was nearly pixel-identical; that does not prove the moving joint, alpha mask, native image loading, motion feel or GPU performance on physical devices. Examine loop extremes, the joint at zoom and at phone card size, the frame break, parallax, app backgrounding and both native platforms before considering the track fully validated. Reject cut edges, ghosting, unnatural stiffness or clipping; use properly authored in-betweens or a weighted mesh if the two-section approach fails.

## Existing character and shared motion

- **Sherlock / Dracula:** `src/data/sherlockScarf.ts` and `src/data/draculaCape.ts` register a character base and three full-canvas pose images. `LayeredCard.tsx` crossfades their registered poses and applies a small anchored rotation. Sherlock's scarf is composited in front with a feathered attachment mask; Dracula's cape is behind the body. Both follow the character transform and stop their card-specific phase outside the selected Unbound front. These are existing prototypes, not a reusable guarantee of seamless cloth. Each still needs two additional card-specific pieces and a quality review.
- **Card tilt and parallax:** gesture and device-orientation input are clamped and smoothed; all evolutions allow card-only horizontal tilt. Unbound adds vertical tilt and independently transforms the background, character, foreground and title. `useDeviceTilt.ts` calibrates device motion, falls back to accelerometer or touch, and recalibrates on foreground return. Layers must stay attached to their intended parent, with environment clipped inside the frame and the character's lower quarter behind the frame.
- **Frame, gems and title:** the shared Legendary frame animates at every evolution using masked horizontal top/bottom and vertical side sweeps, feathered boundaries, corner glints, active gem pulses and crystal twinkles. Sparkles and the top rail remain behind frame-breaking characters; stat shields remain static. Unbound's background and title also have independent idle and tilt-responsive highlights, with the title shine masked to its art. These are shared UI effects and do **not** satisfy the three-piece card-specific requirement. Keep shimmer off character artwork and avoid pale full-frame washes or rectangular boundaries.

## Verification before accepting a track

1. Inspect rest composites at full size and zoomed in; compare positions across levels and during tilt. Check the exact mask, source, attachment, depth and occlusion; confirm a completed base beneath separated pieces.
2. Capture and watch multiple full loops at actual phone card size, including both sides of every reset and high-contrast edges. Inspect for motion that stops, jumps, doubles, smears or moves unrelated scenery; compare the rest pose to Origin and Awakened. Confirm the intended motion is easy to see without covering the face, title or frame.
3. Verify evolution and selected-card switching, flip/back, foreground/background, reduced motion, drag and sensor-driven parallax. Motion must stop when its track is inactive; assets must load and errors must be handled.
4. Run relevant type/build and behavior checks and record what was tested. Check actual device frame pacing, memory, loading, sensors and masking on representative Android and iOS hardware; a browser screenshot, synthetic sensor event or passing mathematical handoff check does not establish native appearance, seamlessness or performance.

The existing browser checks exercised Tarzan's evolution gating, flip, reduced motion and sample handoffs. The current waterfall has not yet been verified on physical Android or iOS hardware. Sherlock, Dracula and Tarzan remain incomplete against the three-piece standard; their existing prototypes and the other proposed pieces require the applicable quality gates.
