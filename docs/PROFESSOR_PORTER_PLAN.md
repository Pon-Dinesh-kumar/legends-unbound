# Professor Porter — Common card plan

## Source and identity

Professor Archimedes Q. Porter, Jane's father, appears in Edgar Rice Burroughs's [*Tarzan of the Apes*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/78), published in 1912 and listed by Project Gutenberg as public domain in the USA. Consulted 21 November 2026; rights and trademarks elsewhere remain unverified. The original text depicts an absent-minded, elderly scholar with spectacles, gray hair, a top hat and a frock coat; base the character on the early novel rather than later adaptation designs. His notebook, specimen and drink in this interpretation are creative choices, not claims of exact source details. Card ID `professor`, title **PROFESSOR PORTER**, Common, provisional cost 2 / power 2.

## Composition and visual identity

Choose an intimate, lightly comic study pose rather than a reaching-hand action: a gray-haired professor in spectacles and old-fashioned frock coat sits in a jungle field shelter, studying an open notebook and a nearby butterfly. His head, hat, hands and notebook remain legible; his chair and elbows must meet their supports. Use a distinct original shelter interior with rolled charts and stormy blue-green foliage beyond an open window, warm lantern light motivated by the table lamp, no default sunbeams. Maintain the collection's painterly animated-feature style and enough visible setting. Keep a clear silhouette for a natural top/side frame break with no Cost/Power collision. Large unique carved brass/ink dimensional title at bottom. The shared simple light-blue Common frame and gems are unchanged across evolutions. Origin is static and inside; Awakened adds only character frame break; Unbound adds parallax and three independent motions. Same rest scene and crop; background fits the seated pose rather than leaving him floating.

## Redesigned direction — planned before replacement generation

Return to the early book's distinctive elderly, absent-minded scientist rather than a generic seated scholar. Compose a lively but believable moment: Porter pauses beside the weathered jungle cabin, absorbed in a curious old field note, his old-fashioned frock coat and slightly askew top hat conveying his scholarly personality. His expression shows thoughtful wonder and gentle eccentricity, not a heroic explorer or another Sherlock. Keep the 1912-derived gray hair, spectacles, top hat and frock coat; tailor expressive shape and gesture to the man. A humid, overcast coastal jungle and cabin have their own muted verdigris, parchment and oxblood palette; lighting is chosen for the scene. Plan his body/supports, hands and title clearances in the complete composition, with the same Common frame and evolution behavior.

Exactly three planned alternative motion studies, all present at rest in all levels and activated only in Unbound:

1. **Field notebook pages:** a short, naturally hinged page turn in the book he is actually examining, with static hand/spine and finished underpaint. Use registered authored frames or a clean page rig; reject doubled drawings, detached page or hand, and cuts.
2. **Frock-coat tail:** a single free tail lifting slightly in a coastal breeze, a detail motivated by the original text's flowing coat tails. Generate it separately with a finished body behind, fixed waist anchor and clean overlap; use weighted deformation or registered poses, not whole-figure drifting. Reject a visible seam or rigid paddle motion.
3. **Expedition clock:** a small clock mounted on the cabin's wall, with a separate anchored sweeping hand, expressing patient research rather than an unrelated butterfly. Generate the clock face and separate hand with a shared exact pivot; keep it clearly readable at card size, static in lower levels and continuously turning only in Unbound. Do not count shared frame or title effects.

Before final base approval, make and inspect these three separate studies, their exact rest alignment, underpaint/occlusion and complete loops, then test them together in the new composition. Create a distinctive sculpted 3D **PROFESSOR PORTER** wordmark expressing antique field scholarship, with bold readable letters and dimensional materials rather than the rejected simple serif. Use the user's feedback as a failure gate: if the figure, title or motions still feel generic or detached, revise rather than returning the old preview. Verify all levels, Shared Common frame, badge clearance, loading, performance and reduced motion as above.

**Parallax correction:** the user reported the separate tabletop drifting away from the seated figure. Remove that composition and its tabletop occlusion entirely. The new standing/waist-up pose has no seated support to detach: the notebook, moving page and coat tail must be mounted inside the same character transform as Porter, with hands/occlusion and anchor positions preserved at rest and both tilt extremes. The cabin and its clock share the background transform, so the clock remains attached to its wall. Only independent depth planes may move separately; do not simulate depth by breaking physical contacts.

## User-requested revision: floor, depth, title and curtains

The standing preview was rejected because Porter appears to float, its title is overworked and poorly aligned, and it lacks a clear middle parallax plane. Revise the actual composition rather than hiding the unsupported figure with the wordmark. Use a complete standing figure with feet planted on the cabin floor, consistent perspective and a shared character/contact-shadow transform; check the contact and tilt extremes in all levels. Redesign the name as legible, restrained, distinctive explorer's field-journal lettering with deliberate alignment and no decorative clutter.

For this revision plan these three independently inspectable Unbound tracks before producing assets: (1) the existing anchored wall-clock hand, (2) a window curtain whose fixed top hangs from a visible rod and whose lower hem moves gently in an air current, with complete window underpaint and smooth registered deformation, and (3) a field-map page on an independent middle-depth plane that turns at a fixed attachment with finished backing. The curtain and map are visible at rest in all levels; only Unbound animates. Keep the curtain rod and its fixed roots with the window, and avoid moving a prop away from its support. Generate unique foreground/middle assets specific to this cabin, with enough overscan, no border escape, and deliberately distinguish their depth from the character and back wall. If a mask or endpoint reveals a cut or the motion is imperceptible at phone size, revise or fail the track. Verify actual contact, depth, loading, loop seams, and tilt at phone and desktop sizes; do not claim completion without three clean tracks.
