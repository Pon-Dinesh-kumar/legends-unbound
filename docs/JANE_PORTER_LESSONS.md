# Jane Porter: lessons for the name-to-card workflow

- Choose character scale and composition for the source and unique identity of each card; do not default to an oversized character, and keep the setting visible where it serves the scene.
- Plan the actual frame-break silhouette and depth around Cost and Power badges before generating art; avoid badge collisions and do not hide them with a foreground overlay that cuts the character apart. Check rest, motion and tilt extremes.

These lessons capture user decisions and observed implementation failures. The [pipeline](CARD_GENERATION_PIPELINE.md) is the execution checklist; this file adds concrete examples. Jane's [production record](JANE_PORTER_PLAN.md) remains the source for her incomplete quality status and asset provenance.

## Research, identity and visual design

- Start from the original public-domain book, comic or cartoon and record source, version, jurisdiction and uncertainty. Avoid later-adaptation likenesses. Jane is Porter from Burroughs's Tarzan books; her custom compass and costume are creative choices, not claims from the source.
- Choose an iconic or source-appropriate pose for each character, with a readable silhouette and natural top/side frame break and parallax. Jane's reaching hand is not a template for everyone else. Design each character's title as unique dimensional art within the established base-card style.
- Design a complete unique scene for each card, including its own backgrounds, foregrounds, leaves, particles, effects and other artwork. Jane reused Tarzan foreground/effects; record this as existing debt and do not repeat it for future cards. Shared rarity frames, integrated gems, card back and universal UI are exceptions. There is no arbitrary generation-image limit; plan every registered layer needed, then measure real runtime cost.

## Three animations: plan before generating

- The Kala prototype repeated a known failure: motion was described before the base art, but usable motion layers were not actually produced and validated first; after assembly, the planned hand was hidden by the title and the replacement underpaint was unsuitable. A written idea is not a motion-ready asset. Before final base art, build and test all three preplanned moving pieces, masks and hidden surfaces in context, including visibility against the title and frame. If one does not work, revise the composition or technique and repeat the feasibility gate instead of completing a static card and retrofitting effects.
- Each future character deserves distinct, story-led animation. Explore custom masks, weighted deformations, independently authored frames, local flow and particles as suited to the subject, and reject generic filler or repetitions used only to satisfy a count. The player review must inspect each track individually and together at actual card size and record evidence; code values changing is not a quality pass.
- Plan exactly three independently authored, character-specific Unbound motions together before generating any art. For each, document the character reason, rest pose on all levels, source-coordinate anchors, parent and depth, attachment/occlusion, finished underpaint, chosen technique, motion path and loop, target phone-size visibility, asset budget and fallback. A frame or title effect, parallax, full-image drift or opacity-only flash does not count.
- Avoid making a scarf plus a prop glint into a standard recipe. Choose distinct movements and techniques for each character's story and silhouette. Consider hair on female characters when suitable and feasible, but do not force it or relax quality requirements.
- Design for meaningful, visible motion at actual phone card size. Ask whether each track can be identified while watching an idle card for a full loop; an animation that technically changes but cannot be noticed fails. Tune range, contrast, duration and rhythm within a natural-looking range rather than using universally tiny movement. Do not compensate by adding cuts, rigid motion or whole-picture drifting.
- Jane's original three plans were hair, scarf and compass; all three now have prototypes, but prototypes are not proof of quality. Earlier scarf/hair cutout joints created a visible hard seam; feathering alone made the cloth translucent. A generated underpaint changed unrelated face and hand pixels, so it was rejected. The currently restored scarf and hair use single-piece anchored motion and still need loop-edge, seam, realism and physical-device checks. Prefer registered separated art and finished underpainting or a tested weighted rig where this prototype cannot pass.
- Increasing Jane's scarf and hair rotation to make motion more obvious exposed visible cuts; the user requested a return to the previous intensity. Do not solve low visibility by merely increasing rotation of a cutout. Before any future card generation, design separated moving art, hidden surfaces, overlapping/occluding joins and a deformation or authored-frame technique that can support meaningful motion cleanly. Test the intended motion range early, and revise the plan if its endpoints show seams.
- Inspect frame by frame at the roots, extrema, loop handoff and overflow, and watch real-time loops at phone size and zoom. Do not pass seams, clipping, flicker, duplicates, detached anchors, gaps or imperceptible motion. Keep every detail in its correct resting position at Origin and Awakened and only animate on the selected, foreground Unbound front; respect reduced motion and flipping.

## Rarity, interaction and verification

- Four rarities are Common, Rare, Epic and Legendary. Rarity belongs to a shared frame and its integrated gem treatment, never unique character art or per-card frames. Keep the cost and power badges in their existing positions. Legendary remains the approved animated gold; Epic uses the shared clean saturated purple frame without white specks, pale areas, frame sparkles or frame sweeps, and has purple evolution gems. Rare uses a shared simpler amber-yellow static frame and static gems; Common uses a static light-blue frame and matching gems.
- The same artwork, title and rarity border serve all evolutions. Origin clips everything inside; Awakened allows the character to break the top and sides; Unbound adds parallax and the three card-specific tracks. The environment stays inside, and the bottom quarter of the character stays behind the border. No character-wide shimmer or ambient whole-picture motion.
- Preload all required assets and fail/retry honestly. Verify visual composition, title, badges, three markers, interaction, switching, flipping, reduced motion and animated loops in the browser at desktop and phone card sizes. Record evidence and known limitations. Real Android and iOS performance, sensors and masking need physical devices; Jane and the existing mobile crash/glitch have not passed those checks. Do not imply that browser tests prove device behavior.
- When asked for a new card, execute the pipeline end to end without treating the prior card's pose, scene, animation selection or frame color as the next character's design. Never call a card complete just because three tracks are present. Record unresolved gates and do not claim the manual workflow is already an automated generator.
